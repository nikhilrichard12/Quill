## Monthly output patterns and energy template

Prompt

Please act as a mix of a productivity strategist and reflective coach. Help me build awareness of how I work best, without it becoming micromanagement.

I want to understand when I tend to be in high-output versus low-output mode—both in terms of the time of day and broader patterns, such as mood, context, or environment.

Here’s what I’d like:

-

Track last month’s output quality, with a few contextual notes (e.g., time of day, environment, emotional state).

-

Answer these questions about me to spot what supported or hindered my focus, things like:

- “When today did I feel most ‘in flow’?”

- “What was I doing when I got stuck or distracted?”

- “What patterns am I starting to see over time?”

-

Create a summary for me that identifies:

- My best productivity windows

- Common blockers

- Repeatable conditions for flow

Tone: Clear and non-judgmental—I want this to feel like an ongoing calibration, not a performance report. Keep it practical, light, and sustainable.

## Monthly productivity review template

Prompt

Act as my personal performance strategist and reflective coach. My objective is to conduct a monthly review to build awareness around my work patterns. The tone must be clear, insightful, and non-judgmental.

Please analyze my activity, calendar, and communications from the entire previous month and generate a report structured exactly as follows, using nested bullet points to separate the summary from the evidence.

Monthly productivity & flow analysis: [previous month, year]

Part 1: Monthly output & energy patterns

-

High-output periods (the peaks):

-

Summary: [Provide the high-level summary of the period, e.g., “Week 2 showed strong, focused output, particularly around product specification.”]

- Evidence: [Provide the specific supporting detail, e.g., “You dedicated significant blocks of time to drafting specs for ‘Alerts’ and ‘Routines,’ indicating deep, strategic work.”]

-

Summary: [Next summary…]

- Evidence: [Next piece of evidence…]

-

Low-output periods:

-

Summary: [Provide the high-level summary of the period, e.g. “Week 3 saw a dip in productivity”]

- Evidence: [Provide the specific supporting detail, e.g. “This coincided with a high number of unplanned, reactive meetings that fragmented your focus blocks”]

-

Dominant energy trend:

- [Provide a single bullet point summarizing the trend, e.g. “Energy and focus were consistently highest between 9 am and 12 pm, with a noticeable dip after 3 pm on most days”]

Part 2: Conditions for peak performance (the “why”)

-

What conditions supported your “flow” states this month?

- [Provide a bullet point for each condition, e.g. “Pre-scheduled ‘deep work’ blocks of two-plus hours in your calendar”]

- [Another bullet point, e.g., “Working on a single, well-defined strategic document with a clear ‘definition of done’”]

-

What were your most common blockers or distractions?

- [Provide a bullet point for each blocker, e.g. “Frequent context-switching caused by non-urgent Slack notifications”]

Part 3: Actionable insights for next month

-

Ideal productivity windows to protect:

- [Provide a bullet point for each window, e.g. “Protect Tuesday and Thursday mornings (9 am to 12 pm) for deep work.”]

-

Common blockers to mitigate:

- [Provide a bullet point with a specific mitigation strategy, e.g. “Schedule two daily ‘communication blocks’ (11 am and 4 pm) to check and respond to Slack/email, keeping them closed otherwise.”]

-

Repeatable condition for flow to replicate:

- [Provide a bullet point with a specific repeatable action, e.g. “At the start of each week, define the ‘definition of done’ for your one to two most important tasks.”]
